## Job Market Paper

**GenAI Governance in School: How Guidelines and Enforecement Shape Student Performance** 

(with Jinghao Jia, Xu Zhang; slides available upon request)

**Abstract**
We investigate the governance of Generative AI in education. Using a randomized controlled trial in two urban middle schools in China, we evaluate two interventions: instructional AI Guidelines provision and Enforcement (defined as a 30% cap on AI-generated content). We find that neither component is sufficient in isolation. Guidelines alone induce moral hazard: male students increase AI over-reliance relative to an unregulated control. Enforcement alone fares no better. Only the bundle generates robust writing gains of 5.1 percentage points ($0.20\sigma$). The violation rate patterns across arms reveal why: for boys, guidelines without enforcement produce the highest non-compliance, reversed only when enforcement penalizes over-reliance; for girls, guidelines alone trigger a sustained decline in violations, consistent with norm internalization. These gender-asymmetric compliance patterns imply that effective governance must solve both a preference-formation and a commitment problem, but the binding constraint differs by gender. Guidelines-only policies may harm the very students they intend to help.
